# Customer Interview Script — Preparing for System-Design Interviews

> Generated by following the `interview-script` skill (phuryn/pm-skills), Mom Test principles.
> Research subject (`$ARGUMENTS`): **how software engineers currently prepare for system-design
> interviews** — to inform **Blueprint**, the AI system-design interview simulator.

---

## 1. Research objectives

- **Questions the team needs answered**
  - How do engineers actually prepare for system-design interviews today, and where does that
    preparation break down?
  - Do they practice under realistic pressure (talking out loud while drawing), or only read/watch?
  - What feedback do they get on *how they communicated*, not just whether the design "worked"?
- **Decisions this research informs**
  - Whether Blueprint leans into voice-first mock interviews vs. a canvas-only study tool.
  - Which feedback (scorecard, reasoning, communication) earns a place in the product first.
  - Which scenario/persona to target first (new grads, mid-level, staff+, internal promo loops).
- **Assumptions to validate**
  - Engineers practice passively (videos/articles) far more than actively.
  - Getting *honest, specific feedback* is the scarcest resource.
  - Practicing out loud is uncomfortable enough that people avoid it.
  - Willingness to pay exists around a real interview deadline.

---

## 2. The script

### Opening (2–3 min)
- Introduce yourself and the purpose: "We're learning how engineers get ready for system-design
  interviews. There are no right or wrong answers, and I'm not here to sell anything."
- Set expectations: "This is about *your* experience. If a question doesn't apply, say so."
- Ask permission to record: "Is it okay if I record this so I don't have to take notes?"
- Confirm time: "Does 30 minutes still work?"

### Warm-up: context & background (5 min)
- "Tell me about your role and what a typical week looks like."
- "When was the last time you had a system-design interview coming up? Walk me back to that time."
- "How many of these loops have you done — as the candidate, or as the interviewer?"
- *Goal:* rapport + their context and stakes.

### Core exploration: Jobs to Be Done (15–20 min)

**Current situation and behavior** (past tense, specific instances)
- "Walk me through the last time you prepared for a system-design interview. What did you actually
  do, step by step?"
- "What tools or resources did you use — videos, books, mock partners, an AI, a whiteboard?"
- "How much time did you spend, and over how many days? Who else was involved?"
- "Did you ever practice *out loud*, explaining the design as you drew it? Tell me about that time."

**Pain points and frustrations** (observe, don't lead)
- "What was the hardest part of that prep?"
- "When you finished a mock or a study session, how did you know if you were getting better?"
- "Tell me about a moment it didn't go the way you hoped. What happened?"
- "What have you tried to fix that? What happened?"

**Desired outcomes** (their words, not ours)
- "What does a *good* practice session look like for you?"
- "If preparation went perfectly, how would you know?"
- "What would you want feedback on — the design, or how you explained it, or both?"

**Willingness to pay / priority** (skin in the game)
- "How much time or money do you currently spend on interview prep?"
- "Have you paid for anything to help — a coach, a course, a subscription? What made you decide?"
- "What would you give up to walk into the interview feeling ready?"

### Wrap-up (3–5 min)
- "Is there anything I didn't ask that you think matters?"
- "Who else should I talk to about this?"
- Thank them, and share next steps (e.g., early access to a prototype).

## 3. Probing techniques (use when a thread gets interesting)
- **"Tell me more about that."** — opens any topic.
- **"Why?"** (gently, 2–3 times) — reach the root cause.
- **"Can you give me a specific example?"** — move from opinions to facts.
- **"What happened next?"** — follow the story.
- **"How did that make you feel?"** — capture emotional intensity (signals real pain).

## 4. The Mom Test rules
- Ask about **their life**, not our idea.
- Ask about **the past**, not the future ("Would you use X?" is useless).
- **Talk less, listen more** — aim for an 80/20 split.
- **Never pitch** during the interview.
- Watch for **strong emotion** — it marks real pain or delight.
- **Compliments are noise** — "that sounds cool" tells you nothing.
